# V1 Mock execution and risk policy

This implements only the durable synthetic Mock ledger. There is no IBKR connection, external order transport, paid model call or Live enablement. The legacy service/repository and transaction serialization remain in place. PostgreSQL still uses the global transaction advisory lock; SQLite uses `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`.

## Admission and valuation

Every BUY and simulated BUY fill checks current account truth, account/market freshness, watch qualification, exact order identity, immutable fee schedule, opposite direction, account aggregate owned plus pending contract quantity, premium/exposure, daily loss and global/linked limits. Opposite Call/Put ownership or unresolved entry on the same account/symbol blocks a new direction across strikes and expiries. Another account has independent ownership, though its risk may participate in global/linked limits.

Allocation, trade-risk and daily-loss percentages are separate integer percentages from 0 through 100. Values above conservative defaults require `elevated_risk_ack: true` in the configuration change. The service stores a digest of the exact effective seven account/group/global percentage settings. A later percentage change while elevated requires a new acknowledgement. Zero allocation or zero trade risk prevents new exposure. The maximum contract count is cumulative owned inventory plus remaining pending BUY quantity. Maximum open contract identities and per-contract premium are independent settings. The global supervisor applies the strictest configured member global percentage; financially linked accounts use their strictest group percentages and require trusted member state.

Current open positions require explicit fresh synthetic option bid/ask marks and an available stock market-data observation. Missing/stale marks or an explicitly unavailable owned-symbol feed block new exposure, including exposure on another symbol or another globally supervised account. `require_open_loss_marks` is a mandatory true policy and cannot be disabled. Quotes are not inferred from execution prices or from a language model. The automation fixture publishes explicitly synthetic quotes; manual fixtures/operators must publish observations explicitly. Account reconciliation does not fabricate market observations.

The conservative daily-loss amount is realized loss for the service's UTC day plus each negative unrealized position result. Positive unrealized results do not offset another open position's loss. Unrealized results use fresh bid value, owned cost basis and projected closing fees. Accepted close reservations retain their original per-contract fee even if settings later decrease. The denominator is initial synthetic capital plus realized P&L, floored at zero; this is not a broker's Net Liquidation/Buying Power or a certified daily start-equity policy.

`commission_cents` is a configurable synthetic per-contract, per-side fee. Its default is **zero**, preserving the explicitly zero-fee V0.5 ledger; choose a nonzero modeled fee for meaningful new experiments. Each accepted order freezes its fee in `order_costs`. Pending premium plus fees reserve capital. Entry commissions enter cost basis; exit commissions reduce realized P&L. Changing the fee never rewrites historical fills. Order-cost rows cannot be updated/deleted through SQL application writes. There is no claim that this fee equals a real broker schedule. Bid/ask execution is modeled by the injected fixture; additional real-world slippage, latency, assignment, exchange fees and exercise remain unverified.

## Emergency, recovery and account isolation

Emergency stop cancels remaining BUY reservations and blocks new entries. It preserves existing owned `SELL_TO_CLOSE` orders. Reducing the entry contract cap does not strand larger owned close quantities. No close may reserve or sell more contracts than are owned. A disconnected/untrusted account requires explicit reconciliation before any simulated fill or new close; an existing receipt may still be read idempotently without changing state. Manual synthetic close fill receipts can be recorded under the existing trusted-owned emergency policy; automatic quote-based fills require fresh available data and do not invent a liquidation price during an outage.

Global and account expiry write-offs require trusted ownership. Expiry is a conservative synthetic full-premium loss, not an exercise/assignment or broker settlement simulation. Historical receipts, old migrations and Git history are retained.

## Reversal and scaling

`reversal_enabled` and `scaling_enabled` default false. They are independent per-account opt-ins for the Mock worker. A manual structured reversal request is also available through the service API. Both paths recompute completed-bar quantitative breakout/pullback analysis and contract selection; no client narrative or AI confidence is execution authority.

A reversal persists one intent, cancels unresolved old BUY remainders, reserves owned closes and waits until the full fill ledger proves flatness with no active old orders. Partial fills and lost responses cannot authorize the opposite entry. A repriced close may have a cancelled original receipt and a filled replacement; the complete reconciled ledger, not cancellation alone, proves flatness. The service then reconciles, recomputes the target signal and option selection from fresh completed data, rechecks current risks/settings/stops, and reserves one deterministic target order. If the signal no longer qualifies or its 60-second intent validity expires, staying flat is safe. Disabling automatic reversal after creation blocks its remaining automatic advance. Intent and order IDs survive restart; restart still marks account truth unknown.

Scaling requires a fresh **net-profitable** owned contract after modeled closing commission, an enabled current strategy, no active entry/close/reversal conflict, a durable position generation, cooldown and an addition budget. Both completed and reserved additions consume the budget, including a cancelled addition; this deliberately conservative policy prevents retries from releasing an ambiguous budget. Fixed or decreasing integer quantities are supported. Each pending scale fill rechecks net profitability using a fresh mark, current enablement, current addition/quantity/decreasing-size limits, the original position generation, close precedence and all normal risk gates. Lowered limits can block an accepted remainder; its quantity is never silently rewritten. A scale intent from a fully closed position cannot attach to a later reopening of the same contract. Scaling is never enabled for a Live mode.

## Exits and decision evidence

The Mock cycle supports stop loss, take profit, trailing high-water stop, one partial-profit stage, configured UTC intraday exit hour, maximum Swing holding days, near-expiry protection and optional confirmed opposing-strategy invalidation. It persists a per-position generation/high-water/partial-stage policy. Partial profit needs at least two owned contracts; available quantity is reduced by active close reservations. All exits are synthetic limit orders; a stop trigger does not guarantee a price. The UTC exit setting is **not** an exchange holiday/DST calendar. Existing V0.5 positions begin high-water/holding-time tracking prospectively at migration; no unknown historical opening timestamp is invented.

Decision snapshots are recorded atomically with accepted orders and rejected requests, including original config, rule version, available quantitative facts, completed input bars (up to 512), contract/request, quote time and original reason. Reversal/scaling record separate intent events. Ordinary manual requests are labeled `MANUAL_STRUCTURED_REQUEST`; unavailable technical reasons are not reconstructed. Snapshots and frozen fees are protected by database update/delete guards. Composite account/order and account/intent foreign keys prevent cross-account references. Intent foreign keys are deferred within the transaction so a decision and newly created intent commit atomically. These protections do not defend against a malicious database administrator.

The functions and database changes are covered by actual Python unit/integration/adversarial tests recorded in the release test report. SQLite tests alone do not establish PostgreSQL correctness; the release report separately identifies actual PostgreSQL CI execution. No financial profitability, production readiness or real-broker validation is asserted.
